# Round 1: educated nontechnical reader

Scope: I read the abstract/introduction, then followed each section and subsection opening, consulting later prose to see whether it restores orientation. I allow myself to stop at formulas; I do not assume knowledge of blockchain implementation or cryptographic terminology.

## Overall diagnosis

The opening architecture and the fund example work. I can explain the central tension: preventing someone from changing or stopping the agent may also prevent someone from repairing it. Sections 2 and 3 now generally tell me what their mathematics is for before presenting it.

The remaining substantial failure is the transition into Sections 4 and 5. Section 4 begins with three technical meanings of progress, before saying how paid workers provide continued operation or what the theorem says in ordinary language. Section 5 begins inside the hardware mechanism, without explaining why the reader is now choosing between verification mechanisms. Consequently a skim gives me a good account of authority, then loses the connection to continuing operation and implementation.

The abstract/introduction state research activities ("formalize," "examine," "derive conditions") more clearly than their results. I can say what the paper studies, but not quite what its liveness theorem establishes. Add one plain statement of the result, replacing some roadmap wording rather than lengthening the paper.

## Suggested scan route after revision

The first sentence(s) of Sections 2–6 should let me tell this story without a formula:

1. The contract interprets proposed updates and checks approval.
2. Verified computation lets the agent provide one kind of approval; governors provide another.
3. Protection depends on every available sequence of permission changes; vetoes can also block repairs.
4. Replaceable workers still need adequate payment and opportunities to execute; finite costly obstruction gives a conditional recovery bound.
5. Hardware reports and computation proofs implement verification with different costs and repair dependencies.
6. A concrete implementation illustrates the division of authority and exposes the conditions still needed.

These changes do not require explaining the formulas to a general reader. They require stating the reason for each formula and the resulting conclusion before the specialist detail begins.
